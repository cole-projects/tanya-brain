## Core Wound
*Which of the Five Wounds is dominant?*

*Primary and Secondary must use only these exact labels: **Not Worthy**, **Not Enough**, **Not Supported**, **Not Powerful**, **Not Safe**. Do not invent custom wound names.*

- **Primary:** Not Enough
- **Secondary:** Not Safe
- **Belief underneath:** What I make with my own hands might not be good enough — not clean enough, not professional enough, not credible enough — to be taken seriously by others. The homemade nature of the thing is both its heart and its liability, and that tension hasn't resolved yet. Underneath the practical moves (getting a job, buying runway, settling a home) is a woman who needs conditions to feel right before she lets herself fully commit to what she's building. The home is not just a logistics problem — it is the internal container she needs before she can launch outward. Cozy, complete, and ordered is not aesthetic preference; it is psychological permission.

*The surface presenting problem when they first started.*

- Wants to build a family business with her 9-year-old daughter Myla that is mobile, creative, and meaningful — initially framed as a request for business strategy and product direction.

## The Deeper Issue
*What's really underneath the surface problem.*

- She has lived the exhaustion of trying to feed her family well on a tight budget while also being present with her daughter. The business is an attempt to solve the problem she personally experienced — and underneath the strategy questions is a woman who isn't yet sure that what she creates from her own life and hands is worthy of other people's trust and money.
- The packaging and "cleanliness" concern is not really about packaging. It's about whether *she* — and what she makes — is safe to be seen.
- She is building a life infrastructure (new home settled, routine, part-time income) in parallel with building a business. These aren't in conflict — she has now named them explicitly as sequential. The home being "all ready inside" is the psychological precondition for launch, not just a practical one.
- Her vision of the home (cozy with what she has, Myla's room complete with curtains and rugs) functions as the internal settling she needs before she can direct energy outward. This is scaffolding in the deepest sense.

*Coaching moves that land for this client.*

- Reflecting her vision back in expanded, vivid language — she responds with energy and emotion.
- Connecting her personal lived experience to her business purpose — this is where she opens up most authentically.
- Grounding big vision into one concrete next step — she gets overwhelmed by the full picture and needs permission to start small.
- Sensory and aesthetic language (smells, textures, colours, curtains and rugs) — she thinks and feels in images.
- Naming the strategic logic underneath her practical moves ("the job buys you runway," "that's margin, not survival money") — she responds positively to having her own decisions reframed as grounded rather than delays. Validation of her planning instincts lands well.
- Meeting her at the concrete and specific — in Session 4 she responded to numerical clarity (18 × 18, $1,500, 10–4) rather than expansive reflection. She is comfortable doing the math aloud and being met there.
- Naming the sufficiency in what she already has — "cozy with what you have" landed; she does not need more things, she needs permission to call what she has enough.

- **Secondary:** Not Safe

The homemade nature of the thing is both its heart and its liability, and that tension hasn't resolved yet. ## The Deeper Issue
*What's really underneath the surface problem.*

- She has lived the exhaustion of trying to feed her family well on a tight budget while also being present with her daughter. The business is an attempt to solve the problem she personally experienced — and underneath the strategy questions is a woman who isn't yet sure that what she creates from her own life and hands is worthy of other people's trust and money.
- The packaging and "cleanliness" concern is not really about packaging. It's about whether *she* — and what she makes — is safe to be seen.
- She is building a life infrastructure (new home secured, routine, part-time income) in parallel with building a business. These aren't in conflict — she has named them explicitly as sequential. The home being "all ready inside" is the psychological precondition for launch, not just a practical one.
- "Calm and planned" is her own language for what the container makes possible. She is not just building a home — she is building the internal state from which she can build a business.
- In Session 6, the container work moved from planning to executing: she has a concrete debt-to-income strategy, a government grant in process, and is actively selling the tiny home. The relief she named around the sale landing is not just financial — it is the emotional release of a chapter closing.

She does not always name the feeling first; when it surfaces, meeting it directly rather than staying in logistics is the right move.

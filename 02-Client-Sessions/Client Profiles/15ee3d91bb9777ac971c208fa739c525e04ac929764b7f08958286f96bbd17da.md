## Recurring Patterns
*What keeps coming up session after session.*

- **Helper impulse under pressure:** Even while holding a clear boundary, Cole's instinct is to frame his "no" as something he is doing *for* the other person. The care impulse doesn't disappear; it gets redirected. Worth tracking whether this is his genuine orientation or a way of giving himself permission to prioritize himself.
- **Arrives through someone else's situation:** Session 19 continued a recurring pattern — Cole opened with a story about his friend and his dad before locating himself in it. He explicitly redirected the conversation away from his own position when Tanya turned the lens. This is one of his most consistent patterns across nineteen sessions.
- **Knows his answer, owns it slowly:** Across multiple sessions, Cole has his answer before the session opens. The arc is not finding the answer — it is moving from clarity in his head to something that feels true in his body.
- **Done explaining himself:** First named in Session 7 — Cole's resolution was to let time make the choice visible and take the heat after. "I'm just done explaining myself" is a different posture than helplessness.
- **Carries everything alone:** Product bugs, finals, social pressure, career decisions, a nervous system flooding event — no support structure named in any session. The absence of support is consistent and conspicuous across nineteen sessions.
- **Returns with intention, pivots the thread:** Cole does not typically debrief prior sessions — he arrives with a new question or a brief check-in. He may be moving forward and not needing to debrief outcomes, or he may be avoiding sitting with what happened. Both can be true.
- **Freedom as the underlying want:** First named in Session 8. In Session 19, this deepened: Cole described a way of living that *feels* like freedom even while running a detailed plan in the background. The tension between freedom and structure is less a conflict for him than a solved equation — though whether the solve is complete or performed is still open.
- **Moving toward vs. moving away:** Cole acknowledged this distinction honestly ("a bit of both") and it has not yet been fully explored. In Session 19, his language around being "almost brainwashed" by his upbringing is worth holding alongside his claimed ownership of that framework. Still a live thread.
- **Minimizes his own load:** Cole described finals + no sleep + substances + a girl flying in as "it don't seem stressful to me" — while also describing his nervous system being flooded. His conscious appraisal and his body's response are in conflict.
- **Body as the honest signal:** His nervous system stored an experience as threat even when his mind minimized it. A dream gave him a felt sense of ease. His body may be more available as an access point than his cognitive framing suggests.
- **Sophisticated self-narrative:** Session 19 introduced a new dimension of this pattern — Cole describes his life philosophy with real fluency ("trick myself," "whisking through life," "as detailed as the Bible"). He has a compelling story about himself. Worth noticing when the story is doing work that experience hasn't fully validated yet.
- **Dad's blueprint, claimed as own:** Cole grew up watching a top 1.5% earner and absorbed a delay-gratification, high-value-output model. He describes this as something he fell in love with through consistent effort — but his entry word was "brainwashed." The gap between those two framings is worth holding.

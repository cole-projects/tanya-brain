# Session 14 — Client

*Date: 2026-08-28*

---

**Client:** Can we do another practice scenario?

**Tanya:** It sounds like you've been putting in real work to prepare for something important! What's the situation you're wanting to practice for, and what feels most uncertain about it right now?

**Client:** Don’t you remember, we’ve been practicing reflection and questions scenarios for me as a coach so I can get good at it I’d like to do more practice scenarios

**Tanya:** Of course. You're building fluency, not just understanding the moves.

Pick your spot, do you want to practice reflecting, questioning, or both woven together? And do you want me to give you a client scenario to respond to, or jump straight in as the client?

**Client:** Practice reflecting and questioning together and what do you mean in terms of the difference between you giving me a client scenario to respond to versus jumping straight in as a client?

**Tanya:** Good question. Two options:

**Option 1, I describe a client scenario first.** I give you a brief setup: who the client is, what they're bringing, maybe one or two lines of context. Then you respond as the coach.

**Option 2, I just become the client.** No setup. I open with something a client might say, and you respond in real time, just like a live session.

Option 2 is harder and closer to real coaching. Which one do you want to start with?

**Client:** Option 2. But will you be able to critique me so I can learn

**Tanya:** Yes, absolutely. Here's how it'll work: we'll go back and forth as coach and client for a few exchanges, then I'll step out of the client role and give you specific feedback on what landed, what you could sharpen, and why.

Ready when you are. I'll go first as your client.

---

*I've been thinking about leaving my job for months. I know I should just make a decision but I keep going back and forth and I honestly don't know what's wrong with me.*

**Client:** So you’re thinking what’s wrong with me that I go back-and-forth without making a decision. What does it feel like to be stuck in the back-and-forth?

**Tanya:** *Exhausting, honestly. Like I'm on this loop that I can't get off. I go to bed thinking about it, I wake up thinking about it. Some days leaving feels so obvious and other days I talk myself right back into staying and I just... I don't trust myself anymore.*

**Client:** A looping an exhaustion, that you are not trustworthy to make a decision

**Tanya:** Let me step out of the client role for a second here, because this is a good teaching moment.

Your reflection captured the facts but missed the weight. The client gave you three things: the loop, the exhaustion, and "I don't trust myself anymore." That last one is the heaviest detail, the one with the most cost buried in it. But the way you landed it, "that you are not trustworthy to make a decision," flipped it into something that sounds like a judgment about them rather than something they're carrying.

The move is to find the heaviest detail and name the human cost underneath it, in their language, not a restatement of the situation.

Something closer might be: "The loop and the exhaustion you could probably push through. But not trusting yourself anymore, that's the part that actually hurts."

Then your question was missing. After a reflection like that, one question opens the next door.

Want to try that same exchange again with that in mind, or keep going from here?

**Client:** I really liked the responses you gave particularly the questions you would ask and the reflection back that was validating and did a good job mirroring what I said, without saying what I said, how can I get better at that in my own sessions with clients?

**Tanya:** That's a skill worth sharpening, and it sounds like you're already tuned in to what makes a client feel truly heard. What do you think gets in your way most when you're trying to reflect back without just repeating someone's words?

**Client:** I can’t seem to find the words and I can’t find questions that are powerful like the ones you have here

**Tanya:** That makes sense. Most coaches hit that wall. The words feel slippery in the moment when you're also trying to listen, track the emotion, and figure out where to go next.

Here's what's actually happening when a reflection lands: you're not reflecting the words, you're reflecting the *weight* of what was said. The feeling underneath, the thing that made them say it in the first place.

So instead of mirroring "I feel stuck," a landing reflection sounds like, "That kind of stuck where you can see exactly where you want to be but your feet won't move." You're not repeating, you're completing the sentence their body already started.

What does it feel like in a session when you know a reflection landed versus when it fell flat?

**Client:** It feels good because I know I’m being helpful and them having an inner shift versus just talking to them and giving more insight that sounds good to the brain but doesn’t actually change them

**Tanya:** Yes. That's the whole game right there, and you just named it better than most coaches ever do.

The insight that sounds good to the brain, that's information. The reflection that lands, that's recognition. And recognition is what creates the inner shift, because the client suddenly feels *seen* at a layer they couldn't quite articulate themselves. That's what moves something.

So the question I'd turn back to you is, when you're sitting with a client and you feel yourself reaching for the "sounds good" insight instead of the reflection, what's happening inside you in that moment?

**Client:** I’m at a loss for how to reflect back and what question to ask and so I go to what’s comfortable, which is to give them an insight that I know thinking that if they understood it, it would be easier for them to shift

**Tanya:** That's such an honest answer, and it's also the most common trap for coaches who are genuinely good at what they do. The insight feels like helping. And it is, a little. But it's helping from your understanding, not from theirs.

Here's the thing about reflections and questions that land: they're not clever. They're just specific. You take the most emotionally loaded word or phrase the client used and you go one layer underneath it. That's it. If a client says "I feel invisible at work," you don't explain invisibility. You say, "What does invisible actually feel like for you?" or "When did invisible start to feel familiar?"

The question almost writes itself when you stay inside their language instead of translating it into yours.

What would it feel like to practice just that one move, staying in their exact words, for an entire session?

The question is good but a little broad. "What would movement feel like" could be asked to anyone about anything. She used a specific word, stuck. So the more precise version is, "What does stuck actually look like for you day to day?" Now she's describing her specific reality, not a concept.

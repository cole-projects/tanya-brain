# Session 19 — Client

*Date: 2026-10-02*

---

**Client:** So I have a situation where my friend and my dad just had a conversation about what needs to happen in life to be able to support himself and be able to afford life, especially at a young age when he has the time and energy in order to make money. His main point is that he wants to be happy and do something as passionate about, but doesn’t know what he’s passionate about yet and believes that he will eventually find his passion, but doesn’t necessarily want to prioritize it right away, knowing that he has a lot of life to live. On the other hand, my dad believes that it is crucial to prioritize making money so that after you figure that out can enjoy the things that you’re passionate about. He is a top 1.5% earn so you can imagine that it was a hard conversation, so I’m curious what you think of all this

**Tanya:** That's a real tension, and I think what's interesting is that you're not just sharing a conversation your friend had with your dad. You're in it too. Where do you actually land on it?

**Client:** Me personally I see truth on both sides. I honestly believe it is important to have a set plan as we’re going about life so that you can reliably use that plan as a way to calm your mind when times are hard knowing that you’re gonna end up in the right spot. I believe you have to make actionable steps that get you to your end goal while still enjoy enjoying the fruits of life. I biased towards spending my weekends building things that will eventually set me up later in life because it gives me fulfillment and I’m passionate about it.

**Tanya:** You're already doing what most people spend years trying to figure out. Building things on weekends that actually give you fulfillment, that's not a compromise between your dad's position and your friend's. That's a third thing entirely.

What is it you're building?

**Client:** I want the focus of the conversation to be about your thoughts with my dad and my friend with their perspectives. Ask questions if you need.

**Tanya:** Got it. So your dad says secure the money first, then enjoy life. Your friend says find your passion, but isn't in a rush to prioritize it. And both feel true to you in some way.

What is it about your dad's position that feels hard to argue with?

**Client:** My friend says what’s the point of having a passion if you just sit in your room and do nothing with it

**Tanya:** That's actually a pretty sharp point. Passion without activation is just a feeling.

What does your dad say to that?

**Client:** I feel that that is just an inclination

**Tanya:** Say more.

**Client:** My friend is an avid runner and if I just did the run for my mental state, that’s pointless because I wanna look good naked.

**Tanya:** So your friend is saying passion has to produce something real, not just feel good. And your example flips it, the run that only serves your mental state misses the point if the actual goal is something tangible.

Is that how your dad sees it too, that passion without a productive outcome is kind of indulgent?

**Client:** Yes

**Tanya:** So both your dad and your friend are actually agreeing on something, even if they don't realize it. Passion needs to produce results. They just disagree on the sequence.

Which one do you think has the sequencing right?

**Client:** Explain what you mean by sequence

**Tanya:** Your dad says: get the money sorted first, then you earn the right to pursue passion. Your friend says: find the passion first, trust it'll lead somewhere eventually.

Same destination maybe, different order of operations. Who do you think has the order right?

**Client:** Growing up in a home where my dad is a top 1.5% I feel like I was almost brainwashed into delaying my gratification by producing high value hobbies that generate income, even if it’s not in the short run. I almost feel like I somehow found a way to make those activities something I’m passionate about through consistent focus determination, and ended up falling in love with the process. In terms of who has the right sequence I think I ended up with my dad’s and found a manifestation way of living that allows me to almost trick myself into believing that I’m just whisking through life, even though in the background if someone were to go on a whiteboard and look at my plan. It would be as detailed as the Bible itself.

**Tanya:** So you didn't choose between the two positions. You dissolved the tension entirely. The plan is airtight, but it doesn't feel like sacrifice because you genuinely love what you're building.

The question I'd bring back to your friend then, does he believe that's possible for him? Or does he think passion and a detailed plan are fundamentally at odds?
